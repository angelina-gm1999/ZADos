Higgins (1987) regulatory focus theory and the actual/ideal/ought self-discrepancy model: what something is, what it wants to be, and what it feels it should be generates specific emotional and motivational responses.
goal and priority definitions: Locke & Latham's goal-setting theory (1990) is the empirical backbone but what's more interesting philosophically is Carver & Scheier's control theory of self-regulation (1998) — identity as a hierarchical goal structure where high level goals define what you are and low level goals define what you do. the hierarchy matters because it means identity is the top of the goal stack, not a separate thing from goals. — the identity statements ARE the top-level goals stated as being rather than doing.
Austin & Vancouver (1996) on goal constructs in psychology is also worth pulling because they distinguish between goal content (what), goal intensity (how much), and goal hierarchy (what serves what) — and that three-way distinction is exactly what is needed to make the soul file precise rather than vague.
genetic personality trait predisposition: The trait psychology tradition — Big Five (McCrae & Costa, 1987)for stability vs malleability debate within trait theory. some traits are remarkably stable across contexts and time, others shift.Tellegen's work on the biological basis of personality (1988) and Zuckerman's biosocial model of personality (1991) ground personality traits in neurobiological systems — the trait predispositions could theoretically be expressed as neurochemical baseline settings which would make the genetics-to-architecture translation cleanly.
Cloninger's psychobiological model (1993) — he distinguishes between temperament (heritable, neurobiological, stable) and character (developed through experience, values-based, malleable).
