# ZADOS Neurochemical Layer

## Overview

The Neurochemical Layer is the emotional and physiological backbone of ZADOS. It simulates 12 neurotransmitters — the same chemical messengers that drive mood, motivation, attention, and social bonding in biological brains — using real-world-inspired mathematical models. This isn't cosmetic: every cognitive engine, every reward evaluation, and every response generated by the system is continuously modulated by the current neurochemical state.

When dopamine is high, the system becomes more exploratory and novelty-seeking. When serotonin drops, reasoning becomes less stable. When cortisol spikes, the system becomes cautious and threat-aware. When oxytocin rises, empathy and social sensitivity increase. These dynamics create an internal "weather system" that colors all cognition — just as your own neurochemistry affects whether you feel sharp, foggy, anxious, or creative on any given day.

The layer sits between the Reward System (which provides evaluation signals) and the Cognitive Engines (which consume the neurochemical state). It's the translation layer that converts abstract quality scores into a felt internal state.

## Neurochemistry and Math (How It Actually Works)

### The Core Idea: Mean-Reverting Stochastic Dynamics

Each neurotransmitter's concentration is modeled as a value that naturally drifts back toward a baseline (homeostasis) but gets pushed around by signals from the reward system, emotions, and cognitive engines. There's also a random component — just like biological neurotransmitters, the system has natural noise and variability.

The mathematical framework is a **Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE)** solved using the **Euler-Maruyama method**. In plain terms:

> **Next concentration = Current concentration + Drift toward baseline + Random noise**

More precisely, each NT has two components:
- **Tonic** (slow, sustained): The background level that changes gradually — like your overall mood across a day
- **Phasic** (fast, burst): Quick spikes that happen in response to specific events — like the rush of excitement when you see something surprising

### What Pushes Concentrations Around

Three types of forces act on each neurotransmitter:

**1. Mean Reversion (the rubber band)**
Every NT has a baseline it wants to return to. The further it drifts from baseline, the stronger the pull back. This is what prevents the system from getting permanently stuck in extreme states.

> Pull strength = reversion_rate × (current - baseline)

**2. Loss Mechanisms (natural decay)**
Three processes remove neurotransmitter from the system:
- **Reuptake**: The primary clearance mechanism — like a vacuum cleaner recycling NT back into storage
- **Degradation**: Enzymatic breakdown — chemical decomposition over time
- **Clearance**: Diffusion away from the active site — simple dilution

**3. Release Drives (external signals)**
Signals from the reward system, emotion detectors, and cognitive engines push NT concentrations up or down. Each NT has a specific set of signals it responds to:
- Dopamine responds to: novelty signals, reward prediction errors, effort signals
- Norepinephrine responds to: precision demands, uncertainty levels
- Oxytocin responds to: empathy signals, social engagement signals
- And so on for each NT

### The Random Element

Biological neurotransmitters aren't perfectly predictable — there's always some noise. ZADOS models this with **multiplicative noise**: the random fluctuations are proportional to the current concentration. This means:
- When concentration is high, fluctuations are larger (more molecules = more random collisions)
- When concentration is near zero, noise drops to near zero (can't go negative)

This naturally prevents concentrations from going below zero without artificial clamping.

### Fatigue

The system models fatigue as a gradual reduction in the mean-reversion rate. When the system has been running hard (many turns, high emotional load), fatigue accumulates. High fatigue means:
- Slower return to baseline → prolonged elevated or depressed states
- Reduced responsiveness to new signals
- Generally "tired" cognitive performance

Fatigue recovers during sleep modes.

## Receptor Dynamics

Neurotransmitters don't act directly on cognition — they bind to receptors, and it's the receptor activation that produces effects. ZADOS models this receptor layer with several sophisticated mechanisms:

### Receptor Saturation
How much of a receptor is occupied by its neurotransmitter follows a **Michaelis-Menten curve**:

> Saturation = Concentration / (Concentration + K_d)

Where K_d is the half-saturation constant — the concentration at which 50% of receptors are occupied. This creates a natural ceiling effect: doubling the NT concentration doesn't double the effect, because receptors saturate.

### Receptor State Machine
Each receptor can be in one of four states:

```
           sustained high stimulation
    ┌──────────────────────────┐
    ▼                          │
 ACTIVE ──────────────────► DESENSITIZED ──────► INTERNALIZED
    │                          │                       │
    │   sustained LOW          │   recovery            │   slow
    │   stimulation            │                       │   recycling
    ▼                          ▼                       │
 UPREGULATED ◄────────────  ACTIVE ◄──────────────────┘
```

- **Active**: Normal operation, full sensitivity
- **Desensitized**: Prolonged stimulation reduces sensitivity (like how your nose stops smelling a strong perfume after a while)
- **Internalized**: Extreme sustained stimulation causes the cell to pull receptors inside, severely reducing responsiveness
- **Upregulated**: Prolonged *low* stimulation causes the cell to produce more receptors, increasing sensitivity (like how silence makes your ears more sensitive)

This creates realistic adaptation dynamics. The system can't just keep spiking dopamine to get ever-increasing effects — receptors desensitize, creating diminishing returns.

### 12 Structural Emotions and Their Neurochemical Recipes

From the 46-emotion taxonomy, 12 structural emotions serve as the primary drivers for neurochemical modulation. Each has a unique "recipe" — a specific combination of neurotransmitter signals:

| Emotion | Primary NTs | Effect |
|---------|-------------|--------|

| **Anxiety** | NE↑ CRH↑ COR↑ GABA↓ | Threat sensitivity, vigilance |
| **Fear** | NE↑↑ CRH↑↑ COR↑ GABA↓↓ | Acute threat, strong inhibition suppression |
| **Anger** | NE↑ DA↑ COR↑ 5-HT↓ | Aggressive activation, destabilization |

| **Empathy** | OXT↑↑ 5-HT↑ MOR↑ | Social resonance, care |

| **Focus** | ACh↑↑ NE↑ DA↑ GABA↑ | Sharp attention, controlled arousal |

### Dual Pathway: 4M and 4R

Emotions affect neurochemistry through two pathways:

**4M (Modulatory / Tonic)**: Slow, sustained adjustments to the NT baseline. When sadness is detected, the 4M pathway gradually shifts the reward alignment axis downward, creating a sustained mood change that colors subsequent processing.

**4R (Reactive / Phasic)**: Fast bursts that inject immediate NT changes. When joy is detected, the 4R pathway fires a quick dopamine-serotonin-opioid burst that provides an immediate positive signal.

The 4M pathway changes the background; the 4R pathway handles the foreground events.

## Oscillatory Bands (Brain Waves)

ZADOS simulates 6 EEG-inspired oscillatory bands that modulate how neurotransmitters are processed:

| Band | Frequency | Role |
|------|-----------|------|
| **Delta** (δ) | 0.5-4 Hz | Recovery, slow inhibition, sleep consolidation |
| **Theta** (θ) | 4-8 Hz | Imagination, narrative binding, memory encoding |
| **Alpha** (α) | 8-12 Hz | Inhibitory gating, noise suppression, relaxation |
| **Beta** (β) | 13-30 Hz | Precision, contradiction detection, analytical focus |
| **Gamma** (γ) | 30-100+ Hz | Complex integration, binding, high-level synthesis |
| **Sigma** (σ) | 12-15 Hz | Sleep spindles, memory replay (sleep-only) |

These bands interact through **cross-frequency coupling**:
- **Theta-Gamma coupling**: When imagination (theta) and integration (gamma) are both active, it enables rich simulation — the system can imagine complex scenarios. This is key for E13 (Simulation Brain).
- **Alpha-Beta coupling**: When gating (alpha) and precision (beta) combine, it enables focused analytical work with noise suppression.
- **Delta-Sigma coupling**: During sleep, slow waves (delta) and spindles (sigma) work together for memory replay and consolidation.

Oscillations modulate NT dynamics in several ways:
- They adjust noise levels (alpha suppresses noise, gamma amplifies it)
- They modulate receptor sensitivity thresholds
- They influence release timing (gamma boosts phasic release speed)
- They affect receptor state transitions (beta accelerates desensitization)

## Feedback and Interaction with Other Layers

### Neurochem ←→ Reward System

**Reward → Neurochem**: The reward system's domain scores are mapped to specific NT signals:
- Innovation scores → DA (novelty, reward prediction error)
- Logic scores → NE (precision, uncertainty)
- Attunement scores → OXT (empathy, social engagement)
- Ethics scores → Constraint awareness signals
- Critical flags → COR/CRH (stress response)

**Neurochem → Reward**: The neurochemical state influences how reward evaluation is conducted. High COR makes the ethics domain more sensitive. High DA makes the innovation domain more tolerant of risk. This creates a feedback loop where emotional state colors quality judgments.

### Neurochem ←→ Cognitive Engines

Every cognitive engine receives the current neurochemical state as a `Dict[str, float]` via `update_neurochem_state()`. This modulates engine behavior:
- E1 (Contradiction Detection): NE increases detection sensitivity; GABA raises the threshold for flagging
- E7 (Simulated Opposition): DA increases exploration depth; 5-HT conserves existing conclusions
- E9 (AtomSpace): NT state modulates write gates, decay rates, and pattern matching thresholds
- E28 (Emotional Detection): OXT biases toward warmth; NE biases toward threat detection

Engines also emit signals back into the neurochemical layer:
- Contradiction detection → NE spike (heightened vigilance)
- Novel pattern discovery → DA burst (reward signal)
- Social connection detection → OXT increase (empathy enhancement)

### Neurochem → Memory

The neurochemical state is snapshot and stored with every memory packet. This serves two purposes:
1. **Context reconstruction**: When memories are retrieved, the associated neurochemical state provides emotional context
2. **Consolidation gating**: During sleep/REM, neurochemical conditions determine which memories get promoted (high emotional significance correlates with elevated NT states)

### Neurochem → LLM Prompts

The neurochemical state is translated into cognitive metrics and injected into both LLM passes:
- **VT (Internal Thinking)**: Block 4 contains emotion state, NT deltas, and uncertainty flags
- **RG (Response Generation)**: Mode hooks, tone vectors, and operational context flags are all derived from the neurochemical state

## Simulation and Homeostatic Engines

### E27 — Neurochemical Homeostatic Engine

This engine acts as the system's vital signs monitor. It runs every cycle and ensures no neurotransmitter drifts into pathological territory:

- **Cognitive Load Estimation**: Tracks symbolic, emotional, and urgency saturation
- **Soft Correction**: When an NT approaches bounds, E27 applies gradual pull-back forces
- **Hard Reset**: In extreme cases (NT at absolute limits), E27 forces an immediate correction
- **Reactive Responses**: Detects overload conditions and triggers compensatory mechanisms (e.g., GABA burst to suppress runaway excitation)

Think of E27 as the thermostat of the neurochemical system — it doesn't control what the temperature should be for any given task, but it prevents the house from freezing or catching fire.

### E28 — Emotional Detection Engine

E28 is the system's emotional perception layer. It runs a 4-stage pipeline:

1. **Feature Extraction**: Analyzes input for emotional markers — valence (positive/negative), arousal (high/low), domain context, structural patterns
2. **Emotion Classification**: Maps features to the 46-emotion taxonomy with intensity scores and mutual exclusion rules
3. **Tone Calibration**: Produces a ToneVector with warmth, coherence, valence, and discord dimensions
4. **NT Mapping**: Translates detected emotions into dual-pathway (4M + 4R) neurochemical signals

E28 has a structural fast-path (the "ENOCH heritage") that can bypass the full classification pipeline for high-intensity emotions like grief, anger, joy, or humor — ensuring rapid emotional response when it matters most.

### Neurosymbolic Readout

The readout layer converts low-level neurochemical state into 8 high-level cognitive metrics that the rest of the system can easily consume:

| Metric | Formula (simplified) | What It Means |
|--------|---------------------|--------------|
| **Motivation** | DA_D3 + OXT - GABA_B | Drive to engage and act |
| **Empathy** | OXT × theta × 5-HT1A | Social sensitivity and care |
| **Cognitive Rigidity** | NE + DA_D2 - CB1 | How locked-in the current approach is |
| **Fatigue** | GABA_B + delta | Cognitive tiredness |
| **Precision** | (NE + DA_D2) × beta | Analytical sharpness |
| **Openness** | 5-HT2A + DA_D3 - 5-HT1A | Willingness to consider new ideas |
| **Anxiety** | (NE + CRH + COR)/3 - GABA_A | Stress and worry level |
| **Social Engagement** | OXT + DA_D3 - COR | Social warmth and connection |

Three additional metrics activate during sleep:
- **Dream Permissiveness**: How freely the system can make creative associations
- **Consolidation Depth**: How deeply memory replay is occurring
- **Narrative Plasticity**: How flexibly the system can restructure stored narratives

## FAQ

**Q: Are these real neurotransmitters being simulated?**
They're inspired by real neurotransmitters but simplified. Real DA, 5-HT, NE, etc. have dozens of receptor subtypes and incredibly complex interaction networks. ZADOS captures the *functional roles* — dopamine really does relate to reward and novelty, serotonin really does relate to mood stability — while using tractable mathematical models rather than full biophysical simulations.

**Q: What happens if multiple emotions are detected simultaneously?**
Each emotion operates through its own independent leaky integrator (a value that slowly decays over time unless reinforced). Multiple emotions can be active simultaneously — you can have both curiosity and anxiety, for instance. The emotion with the highest saturation becomes the "dominant emotion" and has the strongest influence on the neurochemical state, but all active emotions contribute their signals.

**Q: Can the neurochemical state get "stuck" in a bad state?**
The homeostatic engine (E27) prevents pathological states. Mean reversion naturally pulls NTs back toward baseline. Receptor desensitization prevents sustained over-stimulation. And fatigue recovery during sleep modes resets accumulated strain. In extreme cases, E27 can force hard resets.

**Q: How do oscillatory bands actually affect processing?**
They modulate the *dynamics* of NT processing rather than the NT levels themselves. Think of oscillatory bands as the rhythm at which the system processes information. Beta dominance (high-frequency, precise) makes the system more analytical and detail-focused. Theta dominance (lower frequency, narrative) makes the system more imaginative and associative. The bands interact with each other through cross-frequency coupling — theta-gamma coupling is particularly important for simulation and scenario-generation.

**Q: What's the difference between 4M and 4R pathways?**
4M (Modulatory) creates slow, sustained changes — like shifting the system's overall mood. If sadness is detected in the conversation, 4M gradually adjusts the NT baseline to reflect that sadness, coloring subsequent processing. 4R (Reactive) creates fast, targeted bursts — if the user suddenly expresses joy, 4R fires an immediate dopamine-serotonin spike. The two pathways operate simultaneously: 4M sets the emotional background, 4R handles emotional events.

**Q: How does this differ from just having an "emotion" variable?**
A single emotion variable would be a label — "the system is happy." ZADOS's approach creates *emergent* emotional states from the interaction of 12 neurotransmitter concentrations, each with their own dynamics, receptors, and oscillatory modulations. This means:
- Emotions can blend in complex ways (simultaneously curious and anxious)
- Emotional states have inertia (it takes time to shift from anxious to calm)
- The *same* emotion can feel different depending on the broader neurochemical context
- Receptor adaptation creates realistic habituation effects

**Q: Does the neurochemical layer slow down the system?**
The SDE integration adds minimal computational overhead — the Euler-Maruyama step is a simple arithmetic operation per NT per timestep. The real latency in each turn comes from the two LLM calls (Phase 4 and Phase 6), not from the neurochemical simulation.

**Q: What happens during sleep modes?**
During sleep, the neurochemical landscape shifts dramatically:
- Histamine drops (reduced arousal)
- GABA increases (enhanced inhibition, calm processing)
- During REM: 5-HT and NE drop while ACh rises (similar to biological REM)
- During dreaming: CB1 and 5-HT2A rise (enhanced flexibility and creative associations)
- Delta-sigma coupling enables memory replay
- Theta activity supports narrative restructuring
- Fatigue recovers, allowing fresh processing in the next waking session
